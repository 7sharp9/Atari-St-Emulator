Final Fight hit detection

Every fighter carries two boxes. A punch connects when its attack box meets a victim's hurt box, inside a depth slot 21 pixels deep. Everything here is read from the program ROM of the CPS1 game and checked against saved frames of MAME.

Two boxes on every fighter

Each animation frame names a hurt box and an attack box. The game rebuilds both every frame from the animation, the facing and the position, and keeps them in the fighter's record. These two frames are from MAME with the boxes read back out of work RAM. Both were taken on the frame a blow landed: the victim's record already names the attacker and the blow. One frame, in order

The whole thing runs once per frame, inside the main loop, and is built around the players. Fighters and props offer themselves to the players, and the players' lists decide who is tested against whom.

  1. The animation picks the boxes

    Each animation frame carries a hurt-box id (+44) and an attack-box id (+45) in the fighter's record. Id 0 means no box. Bit 7 of the attack id marks a grab instead of a blow.

    animation scripts $3b1c, $3b3c · read in the ROM, seen live

  2. The game builds both boxes

    The record points at its character's box table. A hurt box is an 8 byte entry, an attack box a 16 byte entry: dx, dy, half width, half height, and for an attack also a damage row, a hit type and a sound. The centre is the fighter's x plus dx (minus dx when he faces left) and his y plus dy. A fighter with +97 set has no hurt box, and a grab id in the air gives no attack box.

    $32c4 from $8cc4 (players) and $3382 (the rest) → +112 to +126 · re-derived equal in 51 of 52 attack boxes and 212 of 213 hurt boxes of 127 saved frames

  3. Fighters offer themselves to each player

    After building its boxes, each fighter, boss and prop offers itself to both of a player's lists: victims (the player has an attack box, the candidate a hurt box) and attackers (the candidate has an attack box, the player a hurt box). To get in, the candidate must be within 128 px in x and its ground line must sit in the lane: 12 px in front of the player to 9 px behind. Each list holds 21 entries. A player who is not in state 2, or who was hit by a blow that is still active, has both lists switched off.

    $3382 → $33c4, $33f2 (victims), $3578 (attackers), window written by $8d70 · 115 of 115 active players and 26 of 26 inactive ones match the rule

  4. The lists are walked

    The hit resolver walks each list. For a player's blow it calls $708e, for a fighter's blow $7564. Both check the attacker's attack id and run the overlap test.

    $6396 → $6f6c (up to 21 entries, reset at $6fde)

  5. The overlap test

    Let dx be the victim's hurt-box centre minus the attacker's attack-box centre, and s the sum of the two half widths. The x test passes when ((dx + s) & $ffff) ≤ 2s. Adding s moves the allowed range of dx to 0 to 2s, so one unsigned compare rejects both sides, because a negative sum wraps to a huge number. The y test is the same with the box centres' y and the half heights, and it runs only when x passed.

    $7932 · replayed on 818 live calls: 818 of 818 agree on whether y was evaluated, 63 of 63 y results match

  6. The victim's handler runs

    It stores the attacker (+60), the blow id (+22) and the hit type (+63), plays the hit sound, takes the damage, spawns a spark midway between the two boxes, freezes both fighters for 6 frames, pays the score and queues the enemy bar for the HUD. Which handler runs depends on the victim's pool and kind.

    $70ae table by tag → $736e (fighters), $75d0 (a fighter hitting a player), $70e6 (props) · hit-stop 6 on both fighters, seen live

  7. The next frame, the victim reacts

    The victim's own handler sees its health (+24) differ from the shadow copy (+26) and enters the hit reaction chosen by the hit type: a short stun for the weak blows, a knockdown for type 3 and 7. The blow that landed keeps its id in the victim's record until the attacker's box ends, which is why one jab counts once.

    $a87c, $ace2 (players), $2730a and per-kind tables (fighters)

What a blow does

A blow's box carries its damage row, its hit type and its sound. Damage comes from a per-character table at that row, so the same box gives different numbers to different characters and ranks. A fighter hit by a player takes the damage scaled by his defence class; a player hit by a fighter takes it unscaled.

Rules that are easy to get wrong

  • One blow lands once. A jab's attack box stays on for 8 to 22 frames in the player logs. The victim keeps the blow id in +22 and stays out of the lists until the attacker's box ends. In 127 saved frames 17 records had +22 set: in 13 the attacker's box was still on, in the other 4 it had just ended. read in the ROM · saved frames
  • The slot is thin and lopsided. The lane runs from 12 px in front of the player to 9 px behind, and 128 px either way in x. Jump attacks do not widen it. Only the special (sub-state $10) does, to 24 each way. ROM; wide case not seen live
  • Depth also moves the box. Both boxes are measured from the record's y, which on the ground is its ground line. A victim 9 px further back has his hurt box 9 px higher, so depth takes part in the y test as well as in the lane check.
  • Fighters do not hit each other with boxes. Lists exist only for the two players. The jab in the left frame also covers the knife fighter beside Bred, and nothing happens to him. Fighters hurt each other through props, thrown bodies and the bottle and fire. ROM
  • A box is a table entry, not the picture. Bred's hurt box stays 28 px wide while his arm reaches out; the arm is the attack box. Bosses have bigger hurt boxes (DAMND 46 by 78, a thug 28 by 72).
  • Props have their own lane rule. A prop (tag $a) is a victim like a fighter, but its lane limits come from a table indexed by its kind ($34c4), from 0 to 48 px, with a second and wider set of limits while the player is in his special. ROM; entries not tabulated here

When nobody can be hit

A fighter with no hurt box cannot be a victim, whatever overlaps him.
