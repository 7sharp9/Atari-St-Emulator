# virus: the front end (HUD, text, sound, title, game over, hall of fame)

The code that is not the game world: the three frame routines `$2617a`, `$24956` and `$183f2`, the HUD, the font and text
printer, the sound driver, the title page and attract loop, and the game-over pages. Addresses are runtime absolute; names are
in `virus.sym`. Scripts are in `py/frontend/` and run together as `bash py/frontend/gate_frontend.sh` (about two minutes, from
`scratchpad/virus/{drive,fl,demo}` snapshots). "Proved" lines give the script and the count; "read" is from the listing
(`tools/disassemble.py --snap <snap> --all c000 28000`, regenerated by `py/frontend/fcommon.py`).

## The three frame routines

`$23198` calls them in this order: `$2617a` first, `$24956` after the painter, `$183f2` last.

- **`$2617a` `SpinMatrices_Update`.** Builds three 3x3 matrices with `Attitude_BuildMatrix` (`$1f646`) at `$270ec`, `$270fe` and
  `$27110`, from the frame counter `$32a44` (incremented at `$23198`, cleared at `$230e6`): angles `cnt<<10`, `cnt<<11` with
  `cnt<<12`, and a sine-table wobble. The actors and the painter pass one of them as the orientation matrix (A4) of an object
  draw with the model in A5 (read: `lea $270ec/$270fe/$27110,A4` at `$217f0 $218ea $21a40 $2260e $208cc $20936 $209bc $21c1a
  $228b6 $20850` and the painter `$23e1c`). The code after it (`$261d2..$26296`) is the model-transform helpers.
  **Proved** (`py/frontend/flip_spin.py`, `fl/f1.snap`, 1.2M steps): `watch 270ec 54` gives 999 writes, 111 at each of nine store PCs
  in `$1f68e..$1f6cc` = 3 matrices x 37 frames; control, `ww 2617a 4e75` (a labelled poke, an `rts` at the entry): 0 writes.
- **`$183f2` `Screen_FlipSetscreen`.** The double-buffer flip: toggles `$62a5a`, swaps `$27066`/`$2706a`, writes the draw screen
  `$2705e` (and `$27062`, the draw screen plus `$a00`) and calls XBIOS Setscreen (trap #14, function 5). `$18430`
  (`Screen_FlipNoSetscreen`) is the same without the trap. **Proved** (same script): 37 frames give 37 calls of `$183f2`, 37 traps
  and 37 writes of `$2705e`, all by `pc=$01840a`; the no-trap path has 0 hits. A whole-image scan finds three writers of
  `$2705e`: `$1840a`, `$18448` and `$22fd0` (init).
- **`$24956` `Score_CheckExtraLifeAndDraw`.** Returns if the score `$2708e` equals the last-seen `$27092`. Otherwise it stores it and,
  if the score has reached the threshold `$27096`, adds `$1388` to the threshold, adds one to lives `$32a54` and rockets
  `$32a40`, redraws the HUD (`$247d2`) and triggers sound 32; it then clears the score box (`$249e6`) and draws the score in both
  buffers. It is called from the main loop (`$231c0`), `Wave_Complete_Bonus` (`$210d4`) and the crash loop (`$25b3a`).
  The HUD redraw ends by jumping back into the check at `$24966` (through `$2494e`), so a threshold more than `$1388` below the
  score repeats the extra-life step until it passes (a poke of score `$4567` with threshold 0 gave lives +4). **Proved**:
  `py/frontend/hud_match.py` 12/12 score renderings, lives and rockets +1 in 12/12; sound 32 stop at `$17932` after the poke (one stop).

## HUD

The HUD is the top 17 pixel rows (a 64x64 radar corner at the left is the painter's). `Hud_Redraw` `$247d2` draws per buffer the
frame strips (`$2480e`, `$24808`; span jump tables `$19818`, `$1a116`, `$1c4c6`), the rocket and life icons and the wave and best
numbers (`$248a2`), and the text script `$247aa` ("score" at (80,0), "wave" at (242,0), "best", "VIRUS" at (123,4)), then flips.
It runs at game start (`$23192`), after a life is lost (`$25ba8`), at a rocket launch (`$2530a`, read) and on an extra life.

The control for the digit match renders the neighbouring value: 1 of 36 fields still matches (best 1,000,000 and 1,000,001 both print
`-huge-`). The comparison covers rows 9 to 15: row 16 of a descender glyph sits under the HUD bottom border. The fuel bar (the green
and orange lines under the strip) and the radar frame were not located.

## Text and the font

`Number_Draw` `$24a92` (D0 value, D1 x, D2 y) converts with `Number_ToAscii` `$24ade` to decimal at `$24a8a` (a leading `-`; `-huge-`
at 1,000,000 or more) and right-aligns it to an 8-cell field ending at `((x>>3)+7)*8`. `Text_Draw` `$182ea` prints a script: word x,
word y, then bytes up to 0; `$1f` aligns to an even address and rereads x,y; bytes 1 to `$1e` select the glyph routine from the long
table `$1827e` (index 4 is the colour-masked glyph `$17c4e`); `$182da` prints without resetting the style. The font is `$e9fa`: 10
bytes a glyph indexed by character-`$20` (nine row bytes, MSB left, then a signed advance width), 160 bytes a scanline, clipped to
x<`$13a`, y<`$c1`. `Glyph_Or` `$17b94` ORs a row into all four planes, so default text is colour 15. Codes `$7b..$86` are the
HUD's icon pieces. The digit match above is the proof of this table and the placement rule.

## Sound

`Sound_Init` `$178aa` (Supexec of `$178ba`) silences the YM2149 (mixer register 7 = `$3f`, volumes 8 to 10 = 0) and clears three channel
records `$17b40`, `$17b5a`, `$17b74` (26 bytes) and the mixer shadow `$17b92`. `Sound_Trigger` `$17924` (D0 = effect id; clears the
attenuation `$17b90`) and `Sound_TriggerAttenuated` `$1790c` (D1 = distance, attenuation `($2000-D1)/4`) share the body `$17932`:
they return at once while `$62a68` is non-zero, look the id up in `SoundEffectTable` `$177d2`, and choose a channel: the one already
playing the same family, else a free one, else the lower priority. `Sound_Tick` `$179f6` runs from the vertical-blank queue
(installed by the Supexec routine `$1f86c`, read), steps the three channels with `Sound_ChannelStep` `$17a1c` and writes the YM
registers (`$ff8800`/`$ff8802`). **Proved** (`py/frontend/flip_spin.py`, `hits` on `f1.snap`, 1.2M steps): 100 calls of `$179f6` and 300 of `$17a1c`.

`SoundEffectTable` has 54 long pointers (ids 0 to 53) into 18-byte records at `$17406..$177c0`, nine signed words that the
trigger copies into a 26-byte channel record (words 9 to 12 of a channel are its constants: tone fine register 0, 2 or 4,
volume register 8, 9 or 10, and its tone- and noise-disable mixer bits). The words: 0 duration in ticks; 1 family (only the
low byte is compared, so ids 20 and 21, family `$8014`, never match a playing channel); 2 priority; 3 chain (0 ends; the next
id is the low six bits of the word shifted left two; bit 15 makes a retrigger of a playing family update only the level, bit 14
also ignores it, so 11 and 12, 16 and 17, 20 and 21 alternate and 37 repeats); 4 volume, 8.8 fixed point (the trigger adds
the attenuation); 5 volume slide per tick; 6 mode (bit 0 tone, bit 1 noise; 2 is noise only, 3 tone and noise); 7 period,
8.8; 8 period slide per tick. Ids 15, 18, 24, 29, 38, 50 and 53 are silence records (duration 1, no chain) that stop family
`$0b`, `$10`, `$16`, `$19`, `$25`, `$31` and `$33`.

`Sound_ChannelStep` per live channel and tick: count down, and at 0 load the chained record's words 0 to 3 and 5 to 8 (the
running volume is kept, the new word 4 ignored) and step again, or kill the channel when the chain is 0; add the volume slide,
kill the channel when the volume goes negative, clamp it at `$fff` and write `vol >> 8` (0 to 15) to the volume register (the
hardware envelope is never used); for the tone modes clear the tone-disable bit in the mixer shadow `$17b92`, write register 7,
add the period slide and write `period >> 8` to the fine tone register only (the coarse registers are never written); for
noise, write `(period >> 8) >> 3` to register 6 only when this channel's volume is at least the loudest noise volume of the tick
`$17b8e`, so the loudest noise channel owns the shared noise period (in mode 2 the period slide applies only then). A trigger
takes the channel playing the same family (then the chain bits above apply), else the first free of A, B, C, else the lowest
priority (ties to the later channel tested) when that is not above the new record's. `Sound_TriggerAttenuated` adds
`($2000 - D1) / 4` to the level. The mixer shadow is 0 after `Sound_Init`, which writes it as a word.

Gate `sound` (`py/sound/gate_sound.sh`, about 40 s) runs the game's own `Sound_Trigger`, `Sound_TriggerAttenuated` and
`Sound_Tick` as subroutines through `stserver` from `fl/f1.snap`, watching `$ff8800..$ff8803`, against the model
`py/sound/ymmodel.py`: every id alone on a cleared engine, post-trigger state 54/54 and the register writes of 6,305/6,305
ticks; 40 random 150-tick streams of plain and attenuated triggers (the priority, family and chain paths), 2,728/2,728 and
6,000/6,000; natural play with the model loaded from the live channels at each `$179f6`, `f1.snap` 400/400 ticks and
`g_game2.snap` 300/300 (`g_game1.snap` 400/400 in the agent's run). Controls: noise period `>> 2` 5,477/6,305 and 1,601/6,000
(natural 38/100); every channel writing the noise period 5,104/6,000; priority `>=` 2,647/2,728.
`py/sound/virus_sfx.fsx` (`dotnet fsi`) runs the same model at 50 Hz through a YM2149 synthesis (2 MHz clock, tone
2e6/(16 TP), a 17-bit noise LFSR, 3 dB volume steps) and writes one WAV per id, capping repeating chains at 3 s; its register
log equals the model on 3,472/3,472 ticks. The register sequence is proven; the synthesis (noise rate and taps, the volume
curve, the coarse registers at 0) is not compared with a real ST's output. Thrust (id 5) is noise only at period 30, volume 6
for 7 ticks then 5 down to 1; player fire (id 10) is tone period 248 (504 Hz) with noise period 31, volume 12 down to 1.

## Title page and attract loop

`Program_Init` `$22fc0` sets the screens, blits the background (`Screen_BlitBackground` `$2155a`, the 32,000-byte picture at `$ee32`),
seeds the RNG with `$1234abdf` and `$5678e7f2`, clears score and best, initialises sound, installs the keyboard vector (`$1f842`)
and jumps to `Title_AttractLoop` `$24baa`. `$24baa` is itself the title page: it sets the palette, draws the frame and the text script
`$24d40` ("VIRUS", "(C) D.J.Braben 1988", "Press R to redefine keyboard controls.", "You are about to see Virus demo mode...",
"<Otherwise, press SPACE to continue>") in both buffers, fades in (`$21286`), then runs a 769-pass flying-camera loop (`demo.md`
"How the demo is entered": the timer and its control are proved there). In the loop SPACE or the mouse button starts a human game;
`$1f9e9` sets the keyboard-control flag `$62a5b`; `$1f9c7` goes to the "DEFINE KEYBOARD CONTROLS" page (`$24de8`, text `$250a6`, key
names `$251d3..$25293`); `$1f9d7` held for 50 passes shows the credits text `$244c0` ("Code was assembled on 15/6/88 ...") until SPACE.
All of these are read. There is no separate hall-of-fame page in the attract cycle: the table shows only on the game-over page.

## Game over and the hall of fame

`Game_Over_Quit` `$25bc0` flips, draws "GAME OVER" (`$25c18`), keeps the wave reached in `$32a52`, rebuilds the landscape for wave 0,
silences the sound, waits in `PressSpace_WaitOrTimeout` `$1f7ee` and goes to `GameOver_Screen` `$25e44`. `$1f7ee` prints
"<Press SPACE to continue>" and polls the mouse button or SPACE for up to 1000 Vsyncs (`$18476`); it returns N clear on a key and
N set on timeout. `$25e44` blits the background, calls `ScoreCode_Print` `$260f4`, and inserts the score into the table at
`$25ccc..$25de3` (ten entries of 28 bytes: a long score, a 24-byte name): if the score beats entry 10 (`$25dc8`) it finds the slot, moves
the lower entries down and writes a blank name template (`$25e2c`); it prints "Scores which I have deigned to take note of, so far:"
(`$25c6e`) and the ten rows (score right-aligned at x=`$3c`, name at x=`$87`, y=`$50` + 9 a row); a human game then copies the score to
the best `$2709a` if it is at least as large (`$25f04`) and runs the name entry `$25f3c..$26046` ("PLEASE TYPE YOUR NAME (ABOVE)",
23 characters, backspace and Return, `$bb8` idle Vsyncs; a timeout or an empty name gets `$25de4` "Mr Return" or `$25dfc` "Mr Lazy").
A demo (`$62a5f` non-zero) skips the best update and the name entry and copies the stock name `$25e14` "David Braben / Atari ST".
**Proved** (`py/frontend/hof_match.py`, the page after a demo game over): 10/10 rows of the rendered page equal the RAM table (the `_`
pad glyph is blank, so the table's `--__Acceptable_____` shows as "-- Acceptable").

Order and cost from the last-life stop (`long7.snap`, `py/frontend/gameover_seq.py`, `bp` chain, steps since the previous stop):
`$25bc0` +31,051; `$25c14` +12,623,664 (the GAME OVER wait); `$25e44`; `$260f4` +29,027; `$25e60`; `$25ebe`; `$25f04`; `$25f3c`; `$2607c`
(the stock name); `$26048` +615,401; `$2605e` +12,008,566 (second wait); `$230a4` +12,396,030 (third wait, then `Game_Restart`);
`$24baa` +404,484; `$24d14` +10,677,719 (the 769 passes end and set the demo flag); `$24d28` +532,361 (the camera has climbed past
`$3200`); `$230c0` +141,436, the next demo start, 49,598,717 steps in all. SPACE at the first wait goes to `$26050` (a human game,
`$62a5f` = 0, read); SPACE at the third also does (read, not driven).

## The score code

`ScoreCode_Print` `$260f4` (the line is `$26152`) prints nothing when `$62a5f` is non-zero (a demo), `$62a6c` is non-zero (cleared at
`$230c6`; its setter was not found) or the score is below `$3039` (12,345). Otherwise it prints the text `$173ce` and two decimal
numbers at y=`$1e`: `(((score xor (FrameCounter<<8)) >> 4) & $ffff) + $10000` at x=`$d0` and
`((WaveReached & $ff) or (FrameCounter<<8)) + $20000` at x=`$100` (read). The demo skip is proved by the sequence above (the demo game
over returns at `$26178` without drawing); the purpose, a player-quoted verification code, is inferred.
