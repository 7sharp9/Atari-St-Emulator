How Populous draws the land, and how you change it

The landscape is one grid of small numbers. Every click that reshapes it adds or subtracts 1 from a corner of that grid and lets one rule keep the neighbours honest. The screen is then rebuilt from the grid by pasting pre-drawn 32 by 24 pixel blocks, far to near. This page runs a JavaScript port of the game's routines on the game's own block art. The port is checked against Python models of those routines, which were in turn checked against the emulator.

1. One height per corner

The world is 64 by 64 cells, but the game stores heights on the corners: 65 by 65 of them. A cell has no height of its own. It looks at its four corners and works out what shape it is. The other maps are derived from the corner heights and kept in step with them by one routine, $c0ee.

2. Four corners become one block

Set the four corners of a cell. The widget applies the rule from $c0ee step by step and draws the block the game would pick, from the real block sheet. On screen, x runs right and down, y runs left and down, so corner a is the cell's top, b its right, c its bottom and d its left.

a (x, y)
d (x, y+1)
b (x+1, y)
c (x+1, y+1)

The 70 blocks of this land

Click a slope, shore, flat or sea block to set corners that produce it. The others are not chosen by the corner rule: buildings, rocks, trees and ruins come from the people code, the scatter and the powers.

3. Painting the 8 x 8 window

The game never draws more than an 8 by 8 window of cells. There is no projection maths beyond two lines, because the art is pre-drawn isometric: a cell at column c, row r goes at a screen position fixed by its grid position and its altitude, and nearer cells simply overwrite farther ones. Drag the slider to watch the painter's order, then scroll the window with the minimap, as the game does.

Minimap (click to move the view)

Cell (x, y) is plotted at pixel (64 + x - y, (x + y) >> 1), in the colour $21ea0 gives its block. The game's own click test is u = y + (x>>1) - 32, v = y - (x>>1) + 32, view = (u - 3, v - 3). Blocks above $2e (rocks, swamp, ruins) have no entry in that table that has been traced, so they are drawn as plain land.

  1. Ground. Block shape at lift 8 * altitude. If the block number is 0 (sea) and the frame phase is 0, block $10 is drawn instead, which is how the sea shimmers.
  2. Feature. If the cell has a feature (tree, settlement, ruin) it is drawn at lift + 8, one level above the ground block.
  3. People. A walker on the cell is added to a list and drawn after the whole grid, so terrain never hides a walker. This page draws none.
  4. Earth walls. After the grid, the two visible faces get earth skirts: sprite 77 stacked under the right edge (column 7), sprite 76 under the front edge (row 7), once per altitude level.
  5. No clipping maths. A block whose screen offset comes out negative is skipped whole. Everything else is a masked copy of 24 rows by two 16-pixel groups.

4. Reshaping it

In land mode, a left click in the land view raises the corner under the pointer by one; a right click lowers it. The pointer-to-corner rule is the game's own ($119e6): the screen column picks a diagonal of corners, and the last corner on it whose drawn height is at or above the pointer wins. Try it on the picture below. The four powers that change the terrain share the same machinery: they call the same raise and lower routine, then re-derive the cells they touched.

Pick a tool and click the landscape.

Drag to replay the cascade in the order the game makes it. The routine is a depth-first recursion over E, SE, S, SW, W, NW, N, NE, so it runs off to the east first and doubles back. Part-way frames are never on screen in the game: the heights are not legal until the recursion ends, and a step of more than one level has no block to draw it (the dark gaps).

Earthquake and volcano act at the view origin (the window's top corner), because the game posts the view origin as their target. The swamp goes where the armed cursor is clicked: the cell up-left of the picked corner. This workbench has no people, so the volcano's protected cells are just cell (0, 0), the game's case when side 0 has no leader and the papal magnets are unset; nothing blocks a swamp. The game's rule that a land click only works while one of your own people is in view is not applied here. Mana does not regenerate here. The earthquake's 20 frames of screen shake are not shown.

5. Where the landscape comes from

A new world is not stored. It is grown from a 15-bit seed: three random-walk hills, then rocks and trees scattered in 22 clusters. The same generator uses the same raise routine as the player, which is why generated hills have the same slope limit as anything you build. Drag the slider to watch a world being made.

walk(rx, ry):  x = rand()%64; y = rand()%64
               repeat: raise_point(x, y) until it returns 6
                       x += rand()%(2rx+1) - rx;  y += rand()%(2ry+1) - ry;  clamp 0..64
gen_land = walk(2,4); walk(4,2); walk(3,3)                               ($be84)

6. What was checked

The page's code is a JavaScript port (py/landscape_core.js). The port is compared, on random states, with the Python models, which were themselves compared with the emulator: generation byte-identical to three real $b316 runs, the six powers against callcap on 2670 of 2670 states, the frame renderer on 12 of 12 real frames, a raise at (11, 16) on 134 of 134 corners.
